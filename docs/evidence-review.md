## Primary-source spot checks

**Potassium — independently confirmed.** The UK Kidney Association page lists a December 2023 publication date and an October 2026 review date, but its download is explicitly the **July 2026 updated guideline**. The downloaded PDF heading confirms July 2026. Sections 1.2.1–1.2.3 describe repeat testing within three days for unexpected mild hyperkalaemia, within one day at 6.0–6.4, and immediate assessment/treatment at ≥6.5 mmol/L. Sections 4.1–4.2 add hospital assessment for severe hyperkalaemia and acutely unwell patients with mild/moderate values, especially AKI. The implemented ≥6.5 emergency and ≥6 urgent boundaries are consistent with those priorities; EV-02 captures the incomplete timing/context translation. [UKKA publication page](https://www.ukkidney.org/health-professionals/guidelines/treatment-acute-hyperkalaemia-adults-0), [July 2026 PDF](https://www.ukkidney.org/sites/default/files/documents/FINAL%20VERSION%20-%20UKKA%20CLINICAL%20PRACTICE%20GUIDELINE%20-%20MANAGEMENT%20OF%20HYPERKALAEMIA%20IN%20ADULTS%20-%20UPDATED%20JULY%202026_0.pdf).

**Respiratory — implementation matches the specification; exact 2020 page recheck remains open.** Code uses SpO2 <92 for emergency, 92 to <95 for urgent review, and respiratory rate >25 for urgent review. WHO's publication page confirms the 2020 PEN edition/ISBN. The direct 2020 PDF download was unavailable during this review. An official WHO-hosted earlier PEN edition explicitly contains the <92 and >25 asthma warning thresholds; that supports provenance continuity, not independent confirmation of the cited 2020 p35. Applying those asthma warnings more broadly and adding 92–94 remains identified as local engineering policy. [WHO 2020 PEN publication](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240009226), [earlier official PEN protocol](https://www.afro.who.int/sites/default/files/2017-06/9789241506557_eng.pdf).

**Blood pressure — conservative adaptation, not a verbatim rule.** Official PAHO/WHO HEARTS-D translations retain the higher-care referral criteria around >180/>110 with warning symptoms and >200/>110 without that symptom qualification. The executable ≥180 **or** ≥110 threshold, inclusive comparison, maximum retained reading and immediate routing are explicitly proposed NCDAI choices. WHO's routine adult hypertension treatment guideline alone is not a source for diagnosing hypertensive emergency. The code correctly asks clinicians to assess possible organ injury rather than claiming that diagnosis. [Official PAHO HEARTS-D, referral section](https://iris.paho.org/bitstream/handle/10665.2/57457/OPASWNMHNV200043_por.pdf), [clinical safety specification](clinical-safety-spec.md).

**Low glucose — exact adult source anchor still needs confirmation.** The official Kenyan-hosted protocol exists and its search-indexed material concerns 3.9 mmol/L, but direct document retrieval failed. The repository already qualifies the July 2025 path as a hosted copy rather than a verified publication edition. This review does not upgrade that evidence status. The code's inclusive boundary and emergency classification below 3 are documented operational safeguards and remain pending clinician adjudication. [Kenya Ministry of Health hosted protocol](https://health.go.ke/sites/default/files/2025-07/FINAL_NCD%20protocols.pdf).

## Engineering checks and limits

Every inspected rule supplies source IDs, and the registry raises on unknown IDs rather than silently dropping a reference. Assessment output carries separate evidence and rules versions. Policy-only checks such as duplicate ingredients, exact-name allergy conflicts and missing-data reminders cite the internal safety specification. That is honest traceability of an engineering policy, not external clinical validation. Source records are copied on read, which prevents caller mutation of the registry.

Existing `backend/tests/test_clinical_independent.py` tests were rerun: **107 passed**, with two dependency deprecation warnings, on 12 September 2026. They assess proposed executable behavior, not agreement with an independently adjudicated clinical gold standard. Complete drug-interaction/dose coverage, independent source adjudication and clinical effectiveness remain outside the demonstrated evidence. No grant, pilot protocol or superiority claim is produced here.
