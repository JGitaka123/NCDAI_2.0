# Standalone NCDAI 2.0 implementation pilot protocol

Draft 0.1 · 12 September 2026 · Principal investigator review copy. The owner confirms existing DHA/ethical approvals cover care and medication recommendations and are held in office files. Capturing their reference numbers and scope/version mappings is an administrative documentation task; this draft does not imply missing permission or independent certificate inspection.

## Research questions and design

Can a frozen NCDAI release be used reliably in Kenyan primary care without unacceptable clinical risk or documentation burden? Does the AI briefing add measurable value to the same deterministic clinical and dose support? Which staffing, commodity, connectivity and referral conditions influence performance and sustained use?

Use a 12-month, three-arm cluster-randomised **feasibility** evaluation at 12 proposed facilities across three contrasting counties. Allocate four facilities to each arm: A usual care; B NCDAI rules plus only the approved/validated dose modules; C the identical intervention plus optional constrained AI briefing. This isolates the incremental AI contribution from digitization and deterministic guidance. No clinician is denied established care or access to usual guidelines. The small number of clusters is chosen for implementation learning, not a powered patient-outcome claim. A stepped-wedge design is not the default because simultaneous comparators better distinguish secular changes and AI contribution at this stage.

Select four facilities per county spanning relevant levels 2–4, rural/periurban settings, higher/lower connectivity and differences in laboratory, staffing, medicine and referral resources. At least one county should contrast materially with the original Central Kenya study context; names and agreements are to be entered after institutional selection, not invented here. Do not exclude low-resource sites simply because their workflow is inconvenient. Each active facility must have an operational safety and referral route appropriate to its authorized services.

An independent statistician generates a constrained allocation with four facilities per arm, at least one of each arm per county and balance on baseline volume/resource level. Record the acceptable allocation set and random seed before drawing; conceal assignment until baseline collection is closed. No facility is reassigned because staff prefer another group. Recruitment staff should apply the same consecutive eligibility process in all arms; audit differential recruitment after allocation.

## Participants, consent and intervention fidelity

Enroll adults 18–120 attending relevant NCD care, including multimorbidity and imperfect clinical information. Do not include children or unsupported specialist/obstetric prescribing. Excluded or urgently transferred patients remain in permitted aggregate screening logs by reason; urgent care always takes precedence and a research delay is not acceptable. Use the existing approved consent, waiver and data-access arrangements as applicable, with understandable participant materials and voluntary staff participation. Recruitment, refusal, withdrawal and complaint procedures must be reflected in those records. No new patient contact or enrolment is authorized by these documents alone.

Target 100 unique consecutive active-phase index participants per facility, total 1,200, over 16 weeks, with 12-week follow-up for prespecified HTN/T2D subsets where relevant. Repeat visits support longitudinal analysis but do not inflate the index denominator. Target a follow-up subset of 300 participants with hypertension and/or T2D, distributed across arms; overlap is explicit rather than counted as 600 people. Clinical outcomes are exploratory. Facility sampling and target counts are assumptions to check against screening rates, not recruitment guarantees.

All arms receive standard measurement-quality orientation; B/C additionally receive competency-based NCDAI training covering wrong patient, missing information, acute escalation, dose withholding, contraindications, manual review, outage and amendment scenarios. Usual-care clinicians do not see shadow outputs. B/C use identical dose modules and full recommendations; C merely receives the configured briefing option. Log software/module/model versions, active-session use, exposure to briefing, and overrides. Staff rotation and cross-arm access are recorded as contamination. No automatic algorithm updates or rule learning from acceptance clicks occurs during the frozen phase.

Standalone use must be evaluated honestly: ordinary paper/EMR documentation may remain necessary. Record all duplicate fields, time and transcription errors; do not assert replacement of the legal record or bidirectional integration. EMR integration later is a new implementation phase with identity/provenance tests and a protocol-version record.

## Phases and schedule

| Period | Activity and exit evidence |
|---|---|
| Before grant application/active pilot | Finish product/dose-module development and independent held-out validation; capture existing approval references; freeze release and data plan. Product development is outside the proposed evaluation grant. |
| Months 1–2 | Site agreements/readiness, registration/SAP lock, community and workforce orientation, devices and training; measure baseline service context and documentation workflow |
| Month 3 | 60 consecutive shadow encounters per facility (720 total), output hidden from treating staff; double clinical adjudication and workflow audit; any prospective unexpected urgent finding follows the approved safety escalation procedure |
| Months 4–7 | Active 16-week feasibility comparison; first 20 exposed consultations per B/C facility receive enhanced on-site supervision; roll further only after documented review |
| Months 8–10 | Complete 12-week participant follow-up and 30-day referral ascertainment; interviews and cost collection; close unresolved data queries |
| Months 11–12 | Independent analysis, safety/readiness decision, stakeholder interpretation, dissemination and future-impact-study specification |

The shadow phase tests data capture and context, not covert delivery of unvalidated treatment advice. A research reviewer who detects clinically important danger cannot ignore it for study purity; predefined escalation, staff notification and its effect on the usual-care comparator must be documented.

## Outcomes and data collection

Primary feasibility outcome: during-consultation reviewed-record completion among all enrolled intervention consultations. Secondary outcomes: guideline-concordant care, appropriate dose withholding/selection, clinician decision changes, urgent-action failures, referral initiation/completion, app availability, AI selection usefulness, total burden and cost. Measure all listed outcomes in the [SAP](statistical-analysis-plan.md), with usual-care equivalents where meaningful. Completion and agreement alone do not establish benefit.

Two independent clinician assessors judge deidentified encounter/action records with arm labels removed as feasible; pharmacist review covers dose decisions. Distinguish absent documentation from evidence that an action did not occur; use records plus patient/clinician verification under approved procedures. External records for referral completion and serious events should be verified where accessible, with missing verification reported. Time-motion sampling covers 20 baseline and 20 active sessions per facility (480 total), balanced across cadre and clinic load. Conduct approximately 36 clinician/records/supervisor interviews, 24 patient/caregiver interviews and three county stakeholder discussions, purposively including nonusers, adverse experiences, rural and lower-connectivity settings; counts are targets, with information sufficiency documented rather than claimed in advance.

Clinical and safety data use study IDs; linkage stays under the responsible institution. Records, hosted telemetry, provider requests, exports and research extracts have separate access and retention decisions. External models receive only approved minimized inputs. If verified-source RAG is added before the evaluated release, freeze document/chunk hashes, retrieval settings and permitted output roles, and rerun validation; source hyperlinks alone are not RAG.

## Safety oversight and proposed progression criteria

Appoint an independent clinical safety chair, pharmacist and statistician with authority to pause a module, site or study. Treating clinicians provide immediate care and do not wait for oversight meetings. Log all incidents/near misses and whether the recommendation was seen or acted on; investigate suspected harm even when causality is uncertain. Alert the clinical safety chair/PI immediately for suspected serious intervention-related harm, aiming for review within 24 hours; statutory reporting follows the institution's existing approved procedures and timelines.

Pause the implicated module immediately for any potentially intervention-related death/permanent serious harm, reproduced harmful dose or contraindication bypass, emergency recommendation suppression, wrong-patient/cross-facility disclosure, or loss of clinical record integrity. Two independently confirmed clinically material medication/triage errors within any 50 exposed encounters trigger a pause/review even without harm. Ordinary AI unavailability disables briefing while validated deterministic support remains; backend failure uses routine facility care. Restart needs documented cause/correction, independent regression evidence and safety-chair/PI decision under institutional processes. These are proposed operating rules for PI review, not claims of universal thresholds.

At the shadow and pilot reviews, use a prespecified traffic-light decision: **proceed** only with no unresolved critical safety/privacy defects, intervention completion≥85%, assessed usability/training adequacy, follow-up ascertainment≥80%, and no persistent material burden or inequity signal; **modify/re-evaluate** for completion 70–84%, follow-up 60–79%, or a fixable burden/access gap; **do not scale the current version** for unresolved critical defects, completion<70%, follow-up<60%, or unexplained harms. Interpret intervals and context, not percentages alone. An observed median total-time increase>2 minutes or subgroup completion gap>10 percentage points triggers investigation; these are proposed decision thresholds to confirm with frontline stakeholders, not clinical efficacy tests. Removing AI is an acceptable result if C adds cost/burden without useful improvement over B.

Use DECIDE-AI to report human factors and errors, and the randomised pilot/CONSORT-AI framework for participant flow, versions, deviations and harms. Publish negative and null findings, with independent analysis and conflicts disclosed. [DECIDE-AI](https://www.bmj.com/content/377/bmj-2022-070904), [CONSORT-AI](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-020-1034-x), [pilot reporting extension](https://www.bmj.com/content/355/bmj.i5239).
