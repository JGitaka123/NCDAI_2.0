# NCDAI 2.0: safe NCD decision support and the incremental value of AI in Kenyan primary care

Concept draft 0.1 · 12 September 2026 · **Proposed Pathway-A-style evaluation; not a submitted application or confirmed response to an open call.**

**Lead:** Kenyan legally recognized institution and region-based PI, to be entered. **Partners:** county/facility implementation institutions, independent clinical/pharmacy/statistical evaluators and community representatives, to be confirmed by named agreements. Earlier proposals do not establish commitments for this study. **Duration:**12 months. **Proposed evaluation request:**USD 403,920. **Setting:**12 proposed facilities in three deliberately contrasting Kenyan counties.

## Problem and intervention

Frontline NCD care requires clinicians to reconcile multiple conditions, medicines, observations, resource constraints and referrals during a busy consultation. Decision support may improve this work, but extra data entry, incomplete recommendations and dependence on a remote model can offset its value. The proposed research asks which elements help clinicians and patients, in which settings, and at what cost.

NCDAI 2.0 combines structured clinical review, deterministic acute and medication safeguards, a bounded Kenya-guideline dose-support module, source-linked recommendations, documented clinician decisions and referral follow-through. Its selected adult scope covers cardiovascular/hypertension, diabetes, respiratory and kidney findings, cancer warning/referral and multimorbidity. It does not claim complete treatment of every NCD or autonomous prescribing. The application begins standalone, as requested; record integration with Aifya EMR and QAfya is a later option. System integration in this evaluation means actual fit with facility responsibilities, documentation and referral services; technical EMR connectivity is not represented as complete.

The AI briefing currently selects existing canonical actions and missing-data IDs. It does not calculate doses or generate unconstrained treatment advice. Verified-source retrieval may later supply controlled, attributable context, but hyperlinks alone are not retrieval-augmented generation. Any retrieval component entering the study must be implemented and validated before the intervention is frozen, with source/chunk hashes and retrieval settings recorded. LLM training is neither the proposed novelty nor a funded activity. The hypothesis is that constrained AI organization can improve attention or workflow beyond the same rules; a finding of no additional value would directly inform a simpler, less expensive deployment.

## Evidence and readiness

The supplied original NCDAI publication describes 1,929 retrospective records, 300 expert recommendation reviews, 29 clinician surveys and 11 interviews. Of the expert reviews, 67.0% fully agreed and 26.3% partially agreed; medication/treatment planning and duplicate documentation warranted further work. These results establish formative evidence, not 93.3% diagnostic accuracy, demonstrated patient benefit or validation of this new release. The owner confirms no routine use since the study. [Original NCDAI publication, supplied version 1](https://doi.org/10.12688/openresafrica.16362.1).

The current build has a dated engineering verification record; newly added dosing will have its own tests and independently adjudicated validation. Before grant submission and active pilot, freeze the functioning intervention and complete the 900-case held-out protocol. The project owner confirms DHA and ethical approvals already cover care and medication recommendations, with records held in office files. Reference/version mapping is an administrative completion item; this concept does not claim to have inspected those documents. Participating-site and deployment details are recorded under the existing institutional processes.

## Evaluation and contribution

Following 720 shadow encounters, a cluster-randomised feasibility evaluation will allocate four facilities each to usual care, rules/dose support, and identical support plus AI briefing. Enroll 1,200 unique adult index participants over 16 weeks, with a 300-person HTN/T2D follow-up subset. Facility selection will deliberately include lower-connectivity and lower-resource contexts beyond the original study setting. Independent clinicians and pharmacists will judge management and dose decisions, with the statistician independent of product development. The 12-cluster study is not claimed to establish definitive effects on BP, HbA1c or rare harms.

The primary implementation outcome is completed clinician review during eligible intervention consultations. Comparative exploratory outcomes include guideline-concordant management, appropriate withholding/dose support, total consultation and documentation time, referral completion, errors, patient experience and resource costs. Measure the whole workflow, including repeated entry into usual records, rather than relying on app timestamps or self-reported time savings. Clinical follow-up measures SBP and HbA1c in their own units with appropriate clustering and missingness treatment. Interviews include patients, nonusers, supervisors, records teams and referral actors; frontline clinicians remain the intended intervention users.

Safety oversight includes immediate care, independent incident review, predefined module/site pause rules, version freeze and transparent correction records. Subgroup analysis examines age, sex/gender where approved, geography, resource level, cadre, language and disease mix with uncertainty, without interpreting small samples as proof of fairness. The design explicitly separates the value of digitization/rules from AI, practical adoption from recommendation agreement, and patient outcomes from software-test pass rates.

## Decision use, value and sustainability

Deliverables are a reproducible version-specific validation report; a live workflow/safety/equity assessment; a cost-consequence comparison of usual care, rules and AI; a county/facility implementation playbook; and a justified subsequent impact-study design. The decision is whether to continue, modify, remove the AI component, or expand the approved use under tested conditions. Scale-up is not predetermined. A future impact trial will use independently checked simulations accounting for cluster number, size variation, repeated measures, loss to follow-up and time effects rather than recycling simple-proportion calculations.

The USD 403,920 evaluation budget uses one controlled cost model, consistent role FTEs and an explicit 10% indirect-cost assumption pending funder rules. A separate illustrative USD 52,000 pre-application readiness investment covers remaining development and independent validation and is excluded from the evaluation request. Local leadership and a proposed 98.71% SSA-recipient allocation are planning assumptions to verify against contracts. Ownership, applicable licenses, export rights, maintenance handover and a second-implementer route will be documented; no sole-vendor exclusivity or unverified open-source license is promised. Conflicts of interest and independent procurement/evaluation safeguards are disclosed through actual relationships, not inferred personal associations.

Share protocols, analysis code, synthetic cases, permissible evidence metadata and aggregate findings, including negative findings, under a negotiated Global/Open Access plan consistent with ownership and patient confidentiality. Never promise unrestricted patient-data release. County and national stakeholders should co-interpret findings and receive practical evidence on staffing, commodities, connectivity and referral capacity, with dissemination partners confirmed before named commitments are made.
