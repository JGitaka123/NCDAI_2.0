# Kenya deployment and institutional ownership

Reviewed 12 September 2026 · Workstream 11 · Proposed governance, not institutional or regulatory approval.

NCDAI 2.0 is currently a standalone, synthetic-case application for clinician-supervised adult NCD decision support. Aifya EMR and QAfya integration is deferred until their API documentation and sandboxes are supplied. Hosting a demonstration does not establish readiness for real patient records or clinical decisions. The previous study's full/partial agreement findings do not establish diagnostic accuracy or patient benefit for this version; the project owner confirms no routine use since that study. A validation protocol, pilot and grant concept remain separate later deliverables.

## Accountable ownership before clinical use

The following allocation is a recommended operating model. County, facility and project institutions must name actual people and settle their legal controller/processor relationships; software roles alone cannot assign institutional accountability.

| Owner | Decision and evidence to retain |
|---|---|
| County/facility executive sponsor | Intended service, authorized facilities and cadres, resource commitment, referral network, procurement/ownership and named service owner; written authorization for the specific release |
| Clinical safety lead with NCD clinicians, pharmacist and nursing/clinical-officer representation | Approve each local pathway, rule threshold, medicine vocabulary and evidence version; resolve disagreement; maintain hazards, scope exclusions and withdrawal criteria |
| Facility clinical lead | Confirm measurement equipment, available investigations/formulary, emergency referral contacts, training and downtime workflow; review safety concerns and uncompleted referrals |
| Institutional data controller and privacy lead | Processing purpose and lawful basis, notices/rights handling, DPIA, registration applicability, processor contracts, hosting/transfer assessment, retention and incident reporting responsibility |
| Engineering/service owner | Access lifecycle, supported release, dependency remediation, infrastructure security, monitored backups, recovery evidence, provider configuration and rollback; technical administration confers no clinical authority |
| Health records/interoperability lead | Patient identity and correction rules, export custody, documentation burden, terminology/reporting definitions and later vendor/HIE onboarding |
| Patient/community and accessibility representatives | Understandability of notices, language/access barriers, complaints route and acceptable consultation workflow; participation is recommended practice, not a claim that consultation has occurred |

## Current data flow and operational controls

The browser sends synthetic identity and encounter fields to the authenticated backend; PostgreSQL stores facility-scoped records, reviewed snapshots, referrals and audit events. The optional DeepSeek/OpenAI adapter sends only server-derived rule/category/severity and missing-data identifiers, together with deterministic urgency, for bounded selection. It does not send names, contacts, raw measurements or free-text notes, and it cannot prescribe or replace the complete rule output. Even minimized rule combinations can reveal health context if connected to a person; minimizing a future real-data payload would not by itself prove anonymization or authorize a transfer. AI-provider failure must leave deterministic support available.

Vercel hosting and a proposed external PostgreSQL service require a deployment-specific inventory of actual processing regions, subprocessors, logs, backup copies, support access and deletion behavior. A proposed Frankfurt database region is a foreign processing location, not a Kenyan compliance finding. Demonstration hosting terms must be accepted by the authorized account owner. Before real records, the controller must approve the complete data flow, including browser downloads and operational telemetry, and obtain evidence of encryption in transit and at rest, key custody, access revocation and restoration. Application settings do not establish infrastructure encryption.

The service owner should maintain a retention schedule per record class, with purpose, period, legal hold, deletion/anonymization method and approver. Current immutable reviews are a safety control, not a complete retention/correction solution: a controlled amendment and rights-handling process is required before clinical use. Export access should be logged and recipients documented. Incidents need an immediate internal escalation route, preservation of evidence, containment and a privacy lead who applies the relevant ODPC/DHA notification rules; do not infer that a cloud vendor will notify on the institution's behalf. Downtime uses the facility's established clinical workflow; this release does not provide offline saving or synchronization.

## Legal and standards evidence to resolve

**Statutory baseline.** Kenya's Data Protection Act restricts processing of health data and transfers outside Kenya; the responsible institution must assess its lawful basis and applicable safeguards. Section 31 requires a DPIA before high-risk processing, with consultation/submission provisions that affect deployment timing. The 2021 General Regulations address sensitive-data transfer consent and onward transfers. These are legal requirements whose application must be documented for the actual parties and data flow, rather than a conclusion that user consent alone permits foreign hosting. [Data Protection Act, sections 46–50](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2019/24/eng%402019-11-15/source), [Parliament-hosted Act, section 31](https://libraryir.parliament.go.ke/bitstreams/26434703-da32-46dd-8b82-0bc6735c7dfb/download), [General Regulations, regulations 46–48](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/ln/2021/263/eng%402022-01-14/source).

**Regulator guidance and onboarding.** ODPC publishes health-data guidance; use it with the applicable legislation and institutional privacy review. DHA's current certification portal requests ODPC registration, a DPIA, encryption evidence, document review and system-specific laboratory testing. Confirm NCDAI's category and the applicable process with DHA before clinical deployment. No NCDAI registration, certification, HIE approval or clinical authorization is established by this repository. [ODPC health-data guidance](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/ODPC-Guidance-Note-on-Processing-of-Health-Data.pdf), [DHA certification process](https://certification.dha.go.ke/).

**Current instruments and exchange status.** Kenya Law lists the 2025 Health Information Management Procedures Regulations (Legal Notice 76) and Data Exchange Component Regulations (Legal Notice 77). The institution's legal lead should record the operative consolidated instruments, applicable court orders, health-data localization/reporting duties and their application at go-live; this review does not resolve all current litigation. Do not substitute the earlier draft regulations for enacted law. [Kenya Law legislation listing](https://new.kenyalaw.org/legislation/recent?page=1), [Parliament's LN76 record](https://libraryir.parliament.go.ke/items/e459ef51-6fce-45e2-9266-d4cb376d8e45).

Kenya Core displays version 1.0.0, active 18 August 2026, based on FHIR 4.0.1, while also labeling the page a local development build. Confirm the production package and domain profiles with DHA. Current standalone FHIR export is not evidence of Kenya-profile validation or authenticated exchange. Later onboarding needs verified facility/provider identities, consent/authorization, terminology, sandbox transactions and production sign-off. [Kenya Core guide](https://fhir.dha.go.ke/ig/index.html), [DHA HIE documentation](https://hie-docs.dha.go.ke/).

## Change control and portability

Before clinical release, retain a signed intended-use statement, clinician-adjudicated rule/evidence inventory and hazard disposition, version-specific engineering/security/recovery results, infrastructure and privacy decisions, staff competency evidence, referral readiness and institutional approvals. Passing synthetic cases establishes tested software behavior; independent clinical assessment and prospective benefit evidence remain outstanding.

Every clinical-content change should record source edition/section, rationale, local formulary/resource impact, reviewer and approver, linked boundary/regression tests, release identifier and rollback plan. Urgent safety changes require rapid clinical review and traceable communication to users. Model/prompt changes follow the same regression gate; clinician agreement clicks must not automatically rewrite rules.

Expansion elsewhere in Africa requires a country/district owner, local legal review, national guideline/formulary mapping, cadre and referral-pathway approval, terminology/identifier mapping, connectivity assessment and clinician-checked language adaptations. English/Kiswahili localization and local clinical terminology are priorities to validate with users; this document does not claim translated or nationally validated pathways. Retain shared safety invariants while versioning local differences explicitly.

Decision record template: **ID/date; institution/facility; release and evidence version; decision and alternatives; accountable owner; clinical/privacy/technical reviewers; evidence links; unresolved conditions; due date; signed disposition; review trigger.** Open records should include hosting/regions, controller/processor roles, clinical-content approval, retention/amendments, incident contacts, licensing/ownership and later EMR/HIE scope.
